## 📌 Project Overview

*A Fool’s Quest* is a turn-based board game built in Unity where players roll dice, traverse a dynamic board, and encounter events, combat, and opportunities to gain glory.

Players progress by:
- Landing on tiles that trigger unique encounters  
- Engaging in simplified turn-based combat  
- Collecting gold and purchasing glory tiles  
- Defeating powerful enemies to earn rewards  

The objective is to strategically accumulate the most glory and outperform opponents.

---

## 🎮 Core Features

- 🎲 **Dice-Based Movement**  
  Players roll dice to navigate across the board.

- 🧩 **Dynamic Tile System**  
  Tiles trigger different interactions such as combat, events, or rewards.

- ⚔️ **Turn-Based Combat**  
  Simple combat system focused on accessibility and fast gameplay.

- 💰 **Resource Management**  
  Players collect and spend gold to gain advantages.

- 🏆 **Glory System**  
  A scoring mechanic where players purchase or earn glory to win.

---

## 🔄 Design Decisions & Changes

While the initial concept remained mostly intact, several features were simplified to meet development constraints:

- Removed **player vs. player combat** to reduce complexity  
- Removed **gear and equipment systems** to streamline gameplay  
- Focused on **core gameplay loop** (movement → encounter → reward)

These changes allowed the team to deliver a polished and functional experience within the project timeline.

## 🧠 Challenges & Solutions

### 1. Player Movement Issues
- **Problem:** Character movement between tiles felt stiff and caused clipping  
- **Solution:** Improved interpolation and adjusted movement logic for smoother transitions  
- **Result:** Movement became more natural, though minor polish could still improve it  

### 2. Scope Management
- **Problem:** Initial design included too many complex systems  
- **Solution:** Reduced features to focus on a stable core gameplay loop  
- **Result:** Delivered a complete and playable game rather than an unfinished ambitious one  

---

## 📚 Lessons Learned

- Prioritizing core mechanics leads to a stronger final product  
- Scope control is critical in game development  
- Iteration and simplification improve both gameplay and development speed  
- Team collaboration and communication are essential for progress
